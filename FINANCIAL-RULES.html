How observed spending is estimated

The dashboard's “Observed spending” panel combines imported bank purchase notices and Splitwise expenses into one estimate per month, without counting the same money twice. This page states the rules exactly as the code applies them.

1. What observed spending is — and is not

  • The recurring budget is a projection. Observed spending is a separate, incomplete estimate for one month, shown next to the planned expenses and never subtracted from them. The only place where planned and observed figures meet is section 9: the fixed income minus observed spending.
  • It is not a bank statement and not a cash balance. An e-mail notice does not prove that a payment settled, and these rules do not check whether a notice is authentic.
  • Totals are kept per original currency. No exchange rate is applied or invented.
  • What each amount means: a bank purchase is cash that left the account; a Splitwise paid share is what the user fronted; an owed share is the user's own consumption. A settlement between people changes a debt, not the expense, so it is never counted again as spending.
  • Splitwise balances are shown as the provider returns them; their sign has not been independently established and they do not enter the estimate.

2. Which records enter the calculation

Both sources are filtered to the selected month by the first seven characters (YYYY-MM) of the stored timestamp.

SourceIncludedExcluded before any matching

Splitwise (splitwise_imports, current account only)expenses that are not payments and not deletedpayments (settlements) and deleted records

Pending notices count. A bank notice is imported as pending and stays in the estimate until someone discards it on /transactions.

3. Decision flow

Conceptual diagram These flowcharts restate the branching in calculateActualMonth. They are drawn from the code, not from measured data or real transactions. They show the automatic rule; pairs confirmed by hand (section 7) are applied before it, and their two records skip these flows.

  • owed share counted
  • bank amount counted
  • ambiguous, left out
  • not counted / omitted
  • nothing further
Text version of both flowcharts

One bank purchase (already inside the included set of section 2)

  1. Is it in a pair confirmed by hand (section 7)? Yes, amounts still valid → paired: count the owed share. Yes, amounts no longer valid → count the full bank amount and count the pair as omitted. Either way the automatic rule is skipped.
  2. Is there exactly one Splitwise expense that meets every pairing condition, with the pair not rejected? No → count the full bank amount.
  3. Are the candidate's shares usable — owed share and total convertible, owed ≤ total, bank amount ≤ total — and does that Splitwise expense match only this bank purchase? No → count the full bank amount. Yes → paired: count the user's owed share instead (in the bank purchase's currency).

One Splitwise expense

  1. Payment (settlement) or deleted? → never an expense.
  2. Already paired with a bank purchase (automatic or by hand)? → counted through that pair; nothing more. In a confirmed pair that is no longer valid → omitted.
  3. Are the amounts usable? Paid share, owed share and total must all convert to the currency's minor unit, with owed ≤ total and paid ≤ total. If the user does not take part, the shares are empty and the expense fails here. No → omitted.
  4. Did the user pay nothing, and is there no bank purchase with the same day, same normalised description and same currency (amount not compared)? Yes → count the user's owed share.
  5. Otherwise, if the user paid or owes anything → ambiguous, left out. If both shares are zero, nothing is counted or flagged.

Consequences worth knowing

  • An unpaired Splitwise expense that the user paid is left out as ambiguous, because the same money may already be in a bank purchase.
  • When a pair is rejected, the bank purchase goes back to its full amount and the Splitwise expense (whose paid share is above zero) becomes ambiguous.
  • If two bank purchases fit the same Splitwise expense, neither pairs: both count at full amount and the Splitwise expense becomes ambiguous.
  • A pair confirmed by hand wins over the automatic rule. If the bank purchase had an automatic candidate, that Splitwise expense is left unpaired and, if the user paid part of it, becomes ambiguous.

5. Converting Splitwise amounts

Splitwise amounts are decimal strings. They are converted to integer minor units with the minor unit configured for that currency in the currencies table — the same scale the bank amounts use — without floating-point arithmetic.

  • A currency that is not configured, or whose minor unit is outside 0–6, cannot be converted.
  • Digits beyond the currency's minor unit are accepted only when they are zeros (1500.00 in a 0-decimal currency). Anything finer is not rounded: the value is unusable and the expense is omitted.
  • Negative values (including refunds) are not converted and are omitted.

7. Reviewing pairs

“Review matches” on the dashboard has three short lists. It opens by itself when there is a suggestion.

ListButtonsEffect on totals
Suggested“Same expense” stores a manual pair; “Not the same” stores a rejectionNone while suggested
Confirmed“Undo” removes the manual pairOwed share replaces the bank amount; “Not applied” when the pair is no longer valid
Matched“Not the same expense” on automatic pairs; “Allow matching again” on rejected onesAutomatic pairs count the owed share

When a pair is suggested (all required)

  • Both records are in the month's included set (section 2) and the automatic rule did not pair them.
  • Same currency, and the user's paid share equals the bank amount and is above zero.
  • The date prefixes (first ten characters of the stored timestamps) are at most one day apart. The description is not compared.
  • Neither record is in an automatic or manual pair, and this pair is not rejected.
  • The shares are usable (owed ≤ total, bank amount ≤ total).
  • Unique on both sides: the bank purchase has exactly one such candidate and that expense has exactly one such bank purchase. Otherwise nothing is suggested.

Manual confirmation

  • Stored in import_pair_confirmations (bank transaction, Splitwise account, expense), kept across Splitwise syncs and deleted with the bank transaction.
  • The server re-reads both records before saving: both must exist, the expense must belong to the current Splitwise account, both must be in the selected month, in the same currency, at most one day apart, with paid share equal to the bank amount and usable shares. Amounts are never taken from the form.
  • A bank purchase or a Splitwise expense can be in at most one confirmed pair; a second confirmation for either side is refused.
  • Confirmation and rejection of the same pair exclude each other: confirming removes the rejection, and rejecting removes the confirmation.
  • A confirmed pair counts exactly like an automatic one (owed share instead of the bank amount) and takes priority over the automatic rule. It is re-checked on every calculation: if the paid share no longer equals the bank amount, the shares are no longer usable, or one record left the month, it is not applied, the bank purchase counts in full and the pair is counted as omitted.
  • “Undo” returns both records to the automatic rule and to suggestions.

Bank notices are reviewed separately on /transactions (confirm or discard).

8. Limitations (true in the current code)

  • The automatic rule is exact. A different spelling of the merchant, or a Splitwise date on another day than the bank's UTC date (for example near midnight in local time), prevents an automatic pair; such cases appear only as suggestions, and only when unique and at most one day apart. Duplicate amounts, installments, partial payments and currency conversions are not inferred.
  • Month and day boundaries use the stored timestamps' text, not local time. A purchase made late in the evening locally can land in the next UTC day or month. Suggestions never cross a month boundary.
  • A manual confirmation is trusted. Suggestions ignore the description, so a wrong “Same expense” replaces the bank amount with the owed share and underestimates spending until it is undone.
  • Totals are an estimate. They can be incomplete and need review whenever the ambiguous or omitted counts are not zero.
  • Amounts finer than the currency's minor unit are omitted, not rounded.
  • Pending imports are counted. A notice that was never reviewed — including one that is not genuine — is in the total until discarded. Bank e-mails are accepted by sender address and subject only; there is no sender authentication (see HANDOFF).
  • Categories (saved per normalised description and editable on the source screens) never change the monetary calculation.
  • A changed or deleted Splitwise record is recalculated after the next successful snapshot; records missing from a provider response are kept, not assumed deleted.
  • Deleting an income rewrites past balances. Only active incomes are listed, so a deleted income disappears from every month, including those already in the running balance. End an income with an end date instead.
  • A false pending notice lowers the balance until it is discarded on /transactions, because pending notices count (section 2) and the sender check is in observation mode. The “provisional” count is the cue to review.
  • Transfers do not count in the net or the balance. Money sent by bank transfer must be entered as an adjustment until bill payments are modelled.

9. Real net and balance

Implemented in src/finance/cash-position.ts (pure functions) and shown in the “Real net and balance” block of the dashboard. Nothing is stored: both figures are recomputed from their sources on every page load, so discarding a notice or confirming a pair changes them immediately.

Both use the observed spending of this document per original currency, with no exchange rate. Each counted amount carries a date: the bank date for a purchase or a pair, the Splitwise date for an unpaid shared expense (the first ten characters of the stored timestamp, as in section 8).

FigureFormulaWindow
Net this monthfixed income of the month − observed spending of the monththe whole selected month; income dated later in the current month counts (it is fixed)
Balance todayopening balance + income − observed spending + adjustmentsafter the opening date, up to and including today (UTC date)
  • Opening balance: one per currency, set on /balance with an amount (negative allowed) and a date. It is the amount at the end of that day, so movements on the opening date itself are not counted again. A currency without an opening balance shows no balance. An opening date after today shows the opening amount unchanged.
  • Income: the configured incomes (/income), each counted on the dates its recurrence produces. It is not confirmed by any notice.
  • Adjustments: signed amounts with a mandatory note, for anything the notices miss (cash, other banks, transfers, refunds). They can be added or deleted, not edited.
  • Provisional: the net shows the month's pending, ambiguous and omitted counts; the balance shows the same counts summed over every month since the earliest opening date (the first month may include notices dated before the opening date, so it errs towards flagging).
  • Input checks (server side): known currency; a real date not after today; amounts with at most the currency's decimals (extra non-zero digits are rejected, not truncated); adjustments different from zero.
